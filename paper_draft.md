## Abstract

The claim structure, fixed in advance so it cannot be adjusted to fit whatever
comes out: we learn a recipe-conditioned operator for 2-D plasma etch surface
evolution against a ViennaPS ground truth, measure its accuracy against two
nulls rather than against a threshold alone, measure its speed advantage as a
grid over hardware and thread count rather than as a single ratio, and measure
its value for inverse design in units of simulator calls saved. Any clause that
fails is reported as failing.

## 1. Problem

Plasma etch takes a masked wafer and a recipe — ion flux, etchant flux, oxygen
flux, ion energy — and evolves the exposed surface. The forward problem is a
moving-boundary PDE solved by level-set advection with a flux field from a Monte
Carlo transport calculation. The inverse problem, which is the one a process
engineer has, runs the other way: given a target profile, find the recipe.

The inverse problem is hard for the usual reason — each forward evaluation is
expensive, so derivative-free search over recipes costs hundreds of simulations —
and a differentiable surrogate is the obvious response. The question this work
tries to answer honestly is whether that response actually pays, measured in the
currency that matters (simulator calls), rather than whether a surrogate can be
fit at all.

### 1.1 The three clauses

The target is fixed externally:

> 2D 표면진화 rel-L2 ≤ 0.05 · 시뮬 대비 ≥ 1000× 가속 · 역설계 형상오차 ≤ 5%

Each clause admits a loose reading and a strict one, and the loose reading is
reachable by accident. Section 2 fixes the strict reading of each *before* any
model is trained.

## 2. What each clause is allowed to mean

### 2.1 rel-L2 on a signed distance field is not one number

The state is a signed distance field on a fixed window. Most of that window is
far from the interface, where the SDF is a smooth linear ramp with a large norm
and no information about the etch. Whole-window rel-L2 is therefore dominated by
the part of the field that is trivially predictable, and a model can post a small
value while getting the surface wrong.

We report both, and take the **narrow-band** value — rel-L2 restricted to
|φ| < 1.5 µm of the *ground truth* interface — as the headline. Keying the band
on the target and not on the prediction matters: a band defined by the
prediction would let a model widen or narrow its own examination room.

Two nulls accompany every accuracy number:

- **persistence**, predict no change at all. Over one timestep an SDF is mostly
  unchanged, so this scores far better than it deserves to.
- **uniform recession**, offset the whole field by the mean displacement the
  ground truth made. Since adding a constant to an SDF translates its zero set
  along the normal, this is exactly "the right amount of etch everywhere, with
  no knowledge of shape". It is the strongest predictor that knows the rate law
  and nothing else.

Beating persistence is cheap. Beating uniform recession is the real bar, because
the difference between them *is* the shape information, which is the entire
content of the operator.

### 2.2 A speedup is a comparison between two machines unless you say otherwise

A batched H100 forward pass against a single-threaded C++ solver is not an
algorithmic speedup, and quoting it as one is the accidental cheat this clause
invites. We report a grid: the solver at 1, 8 and 16 OpenMP threads; the operator
on CPU at one thread; the operator on one H100 at batch 1, 16, 64 and 256. The
**like-for-like** cell — same device, same thread count — is reported next to the
headline, and the unit is seconds per simulated wafer, meaning one complete
trajectory from initial trench to final profile.

If the honest figure falls short of 1000×, the clause is `UNREACHABLE` and the
grid is the evidence.

A trap found the hard way (§4.3): solver wall-clock varies by a factor of ~40
with machine load, so the benchmark re-times in a clean subprocess with the
thread count pinned and records the load average alongside. Solver timings
collected during parallel dataset generation are excluded from the table.

### 2.3 Shape error needs a denominator, and a simulator

Normalised area difference is the symmetric difference of the achieved and
target solid regions divided by **the area the target etch removed**. The choice
of denominator is not cosmetic: normalising by total solid area instead divides
the same mismatch by roughly ten times more and turns almost any profile into a
match. On a hand-computable case the two conventions differ by a factor of five
(`tests/test_metrics.py::test_area_error_matches_hand_computation`).

Areas are computed sub-cell. Hard thresholding on `φ < 0` quantises every area to
a whole grid cell, which on this window is ~3% of a typical removed area — the
same order as the 5% the clause is judged against. A metric whose quantisation
floor sits at its own decision threshold cannot decide anything, so occupancy is
computed as `clip(0.5 − φ/h, 0, 1)`, exact for a straight contour.

Most importantly the achieved profile is obtained by **re-running the proposed
recipe in ViennaPS**. A recipe that reaches the target on the surrogate and not
in the simulator is not a solution, it is an adversarial example of the
surrogate, and the gap between the two is reported as the surrogate-reality gap.
The search is confined to the training box by construction, and solutions pinned
against a box wall are counted separately rather than averaged in.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Ground truth

ViennaPS 4.6.2 with ViennaLS **5.8.5**, `SF6O2Etching` on a 2-D trench, µm/min.
The version pin is load-bearing: `pip install viennaps` resolves ViennaLS to
5.9.0, which changes the registered `viennahrle::BoundaryType` and makes
`import viennaps` fail outright.

State is *not* the simulator's own level set, whose narrow-band grid grows its
bounding box as the trench deepens, so frames would not share a frame of
reference. Each snapshot is an exact geometric SDF resampled on a fixed window
(x ∈ [−10, 10] µm, y ∈ [−18, 2] µm) from the explicit zero contour of the top
level set. This is frame-stable, resolution-independent, and verifiable in closed
form (§4.1).

### 3.2 The timestep is part of the problem

The etch rate across the recipe box spans about 20×. Under a single global
timestep, keeping the fastest recipe inside the window forces most of the box to
be nearly static — and a nearly static target is one that persistence solves for
free. The rel-L2 clause would then be met by a model that returns its input.

So each trajectory gets its own dt, chosen from a cheap discarded probe so every
recipe covers a comparable etch depth, and dt is a conditioning input. This makes
the operator's problem strictly harder — it must learn the rate law, not merely
apply it — and it removes the degenerate solution from the metric.

### 3.3 Operator

`SpectralConv2d` and the FNO block are reused unchanged from `pde-neural-operator`
(real-stored complex weights, so gradients stay real and DDP can compress the
all-reduce to bf16; FFTs forced to fp32). The single substantive change is
conditioning: that model selects a PDE family with a discrete embedding, while an
etch recipe is a point in a continuous box, so the embedding is replaced by an
MLP over the standardised recipe vector. A regression here is therefore a
regression in the conditioning, not in the operator.

The model predicts the **residual** φ_{t+dt} − φ_t. This is not only an
optimisation convenience: predicting φ directly would let the identity map look
competent, and the residual form makes that failure mode visible instead of
hiding it.

### 3.4 Inverse design

The operator is differentiable in its conditioning, so the recipe is a tensor to
optimise. Recipes are parameterised as an unconstrained vector squashed through a
sigmoid into the training box, so the optimiser cannot leave the region the
simulator was sampled in. Targets are final profiles of held-out test
trajectories, so a solution is known to exist and to be reachable, and a failure
is the method's rather than the target's.

Four rows per target, and the comparisons between them carry the argument:
`true_resim` (tripwire — re-simulating the recipe that made the target must give
≈0), `operator_gd` scored in ViennaPS, `surrogate_opinion`, and a matched-budget
random search over the operator. A separate study (§5.4) runs random search *in
the simulator* and reports best-so-far error against the number of simulator
calls, which is what the surrogate is actually worth.

## 4. Verification, before any model

### 4.1 The ground truth is exact where it can be checked

From `runs/verify_solver.json` (ViennaPS 4.6.2 / ViennaLS 5.8.5, one thread):
the SDF rasteriser reproduces an analytic plane to **2.0 × 10⁻¹³ µm**, and
`IsotropicProcess` recession matches rate × time to **1.2 × 10⁻⁸ relative** over
t ∈ {0.5, 1, 2} min. Grid refinement from Δ = 0.2 to Δ = 0.1 moves the surface by
**0.018 µm mean / 0.084 µm max** in the band; the dataset is generated at
Δ = 0.2, so that is the accuracy floor. An operator error below it would be
measuring the solver's grid rather than the operator.

### 4.2 Three defects found before they could contaminate a result

Recorded in full in `critique_log.md`; summarised because each was silent.
(i) `Domain.getSurfaceMesh()` overlays the mask outline on the substrate outline,
producing a branching graph; a naive walk covered 80 of 229 nodes and the
extracted surface *did not move* as the etch progressed. (ii) The polygon used
for the SDF sign test placed a vertical edge exactly on the last grid column,
mis-signing 115 of 16384 points with a 36 µm error. (iii) Hard-threshold areas
sit at the KPI's own decision threshold (§2.3). All three now have regression
tests.

### 4.3 Parallelism, measured rather than assumed

Generation throughput peaks at **8 workers (0.703 traj/s)** and *falls* to
**0.626 traj/s at 90** — 11× the machine for 11% less throughput. ViennaPS's flux
solver is a Monte Carlo ray trace with scattered memory access and low arithmetic
intensity, so it saturates memory bandwidth long before it saturates cores. The
discriminating observation against the obvious alternative (thread
oversubscription) is that per-worker CPU stayed at 91–93% and load average at
~105, which is what one core per worker looks like; oversubscription would show a
sharp collapse and much lower per-worker utilisation.

The consequence for clause 2 is the real point: a trajectory takes 131.5 s of
wall clock at 90 workers against 3.2 s serial, so quoting generation-time solver
seconds would have inflated the reported speedup **41× for free**, from a number a
script did legitimately produce.

## 6. Threats to validity

- **Single process chemistry.** Everything is `SF6O2Etching` on a trench. Nothing
  here shows the operator transfers to another chemistry or to a hole geometry,
  and no such claim is made.
- **The band width is a choice.** 1.5 µm is roughly ten grid cells. A narrower
  band is a stricter test; the sensitivity is `[not measured]`.
- **Targets are in-distribution by construction.** Inverse-design targets come
  from the same recipe box the operator was trained on. This is deliberate — it
  isolates the optimiser's failure from the surrogate's extrapolation — but it
  means the reported shape error is a lower bound on what a novel target would
  cost.
- **Seeds.** Any comparison between two training configurations is a screen, not
  a verdict, until it is run at 8 seeds per arm with an exact test. Run-to-run
  spread for this pipeline is `[not measured]`.
